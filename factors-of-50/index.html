About 50

Half of 100. 50 is twice a square, 2 × 25.

Factor pairs of 50

A factor pair is two numbers that multiply to give 50: 1 × 50, 2 × 25 and 5 × 10. Each pair is a way of arranging 50 things in equal rows, so 50 tiles can be laid out as a rectangle in 3 different shapes.

How to find the factors of 50 by hand

Divide 50 by 1, 2, 3 and so on. Each time the division is exact, you have found two factors at once: the divisor and the answer. You can stop at 7, because the next number, 8, gives 8 × 8 = 64, which is more than 50: any larger factor has already turned up as the partner of a smaller one.

Reading down the left side: 50 = 2 × 5 × 5, which is written 2 × 52 with powers. Its prime factors are 2 and 5.

Counting the factors without listing them

Add 1 to each power in the prime factorisation and multiply the results: (1 + 1) × (2 + 1) = 6. This works because a factor of 50 can use each prime anywhere from zero times up to its full power.

The 6 factors add up to 93. Leaving out 50 itself, the rest add up to 43, which is less than 50, so 50 is a deficient number. Of its factors, 3 are odd: 1, 5 and 25.

Divisibility rules applied to 50

The quick tests agree with the factor list above.

  • By 2: yes. The last digit is 0, which is even.
  • By 3: no. The digits add up to 5 + 0 = 5, which is not a multiple of 3.
  • By 4: no. The last two digits make 50, and 50 ÷ 4 leaves remainder 2.
  • By 5: yes. The last digit is 0.
  • By 6: no. A number divides by 6 when it divides by both 2 and 3. Here the test for 3 fails.
  • By 8: no. 50 ÷ 8 leaves remainder 2.
  • By 9: no. The digits add up to 5, which is not a multiple of 9.
  • By 10: yes. The last digit is 0.
  • By 11: no. Adding alternate digits from the right gives 0 and 5. Their difference is 5, which is not 0 or a multiple of 11.

HCF and LCM with 50

50 and 48. 50 = 2 × 52 and 48 = 24 × 3. The primes they share, each with its smaller power, give the HCF: 2. Every prime with its larger power gives the LCM: 1200. By division: 50 = 48 × 1 + 2; 48 = 2 × 24 + 0, so the last divisor, 2, is the HCF. Check: 2 × 1200 = 2400 = 50 × 48.

50 and 54. 50 = 2 × 52 and 54 = 2 × 33. The primes they share, each with its smaller power, give the HCF: 2. Every prime with its larger power gives the LCM: 1350. By division: 54 = 50 × 1 + 4; 50 = 4 × 12 + 2; 4 = 2 × 2 + 0, so the last divisor, 2, is the HCF. Check: 2 × 1350 = 2700 = 50 × 54.

Multiples of 50

Factors divide a number. Multiples are what you get by multiplying it. The first ten multiples of 50 are 50, 100, 150, 200, 250, 300, 350, 400, 450 and 500. A number has a fixed count of factors but endless multiples.

Common questions

What are the factors of 50?

The factors of 50 are 1, 2, 5, 10, 25 and 50. There are 6 of them.

What is the prime factorisation of 50?

50 = 2 × 5 × 5.

Is 50 a perfect square?

No. 7 × 7 = 49 and 8 × 8 = 64, and 50 lies between them.

What is the greatest factor of 50 other than itself?

25. Divide 50 by its smallest prime factor, 2, to get it.
